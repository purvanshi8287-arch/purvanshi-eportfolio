# Indian Corporate Debt Market

## Five-year performance review, FY2021–22 to FY2025–26

This Financial Market Institutions project examines the size and growth of India’s corporate bond market, primary-market mobilisation, fresh issuance, secondary trading, FPI participation, and the concentration of issuance by placement type and credit rating.

I explored NSE and BSE market information alongside SEBI and RBI publications, organized a five-year comparison in Excel, and wrote a Word report interpreting the trends. The workbook’s source notes also cite the Economic Survey, MoSPI GDP data, and NSDL’s FPI Monitor.

## Key findings in the report

- Outstanding corporate bonds increased from ₹40.2 lakh crore in FY22 to ₹58.2 lakh crore in FY26, a reported four-year CAGR of about 9.7%. Market size stayed within roughly 15–17% of nominal GDP.
- Debt supplied 75.4% of the combined equity and debt raised in the primary market over the five years.
- Fresh issuance peaked at ₹9.95 lakh crore in FY25, then was about ₹9.10 lakh crore in FY26.
- Average daily secondary-market turnover was about ₹6,674 crore across the five years.
- Private placements represented about 98.6% of issuance. The rating figures combine AAA and AA+ (and above); they do not establish the AAA-only share.
- Corporate-bond FPI holdings stayed near ₹1.04–1.25 lakh crore. The net FPI flow series in the workbook covers all debt, not corporate bonds alone.

## Evidence and limitations

FY26 figures are provisional or approximate. The workbook notes that equity mobilisation, turnover, settled trades, RFQ volumes, FPI flows, and rating shares still need to be checked against their original tables. Changes in corporate-bond FPI holdings can also include price changes.
